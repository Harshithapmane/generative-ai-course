# Day 2 — The Tools: Chat Assistants, Coding Assistants, and More

The tools most people meet first are chat assistants: a text box where you type a question or request and get a written reply. Behind that box is an LLM. The three you will hear about most are:

- **ChatGPT**, made by OpenAI. The one that made this technology mainstream.
- **Claude**, made by Anthropic. Known for careful, longer-form writing and working with big documents.
- **Gemini**, made by Google. Built into Google's products and search.

There are others, but these three are the general-purpose assistants a lot of people use daily.

## They Are More Alike Than Different

It is easy to think of these as completely separate products, but under the hood they are the same idea: a large language model with a chat interface wrapped around it. You type, it generates a reply, you type again. Each has its own personality and strengths, and which one is "best" shifts every few months as new versions come out.

For a beginner, the practical takeaway is reassuring. Learn to work with one and you can work with all of them, because the way you ask - the actual skill this course teaches - is the same across every chat assistant.

## The Names Will Change

Do not get too attached to any single product name or version number. This field moves fast. New models arrive constantly, leaders trade places, and today's best choice may not be next year's. The durable skill is understanding what these tools are and how to use them well, which is exactly what the rest of the course builds. Next: the tools that are not just a chat box.

A chat box is only one shape AI comes in. The same LLM technology shows up in several other places, packaged for a specific job. Three shapes are worth knowing.

## Coding Assistants

These live inside a code editor or terminal and help you write, fix, and explain code. You describe what you want in plain language and they produce it right where you work.

- **GitHub Copilot** - suggestions inside your editor as you type.
- **Cursor** - an editor built around AI.
- **Claude Code** - an assistant that works in your terminal.

This course has a whole topic on using one of these later, so this is just the map for now.

## AI Search

Regular search gives you links. AI search reads the web for you and writes an answer, with sources to check. Perplexity is the best-known example. It is handy when you want a researched answer rather than a list of pages.

## AI Built Into Apps You Already Use

More and more, the AI comes to you. It is being built directly into everyday software: Google Docs and Microsoft Office can draft and rewrite text, Notion can summarize your notes, and email apps can suggest replies. You often do not pick a separate tool at all - the assistant is just a button inside the app.

## Same Engine, Different Packaging

Chat box, editor plugin, search engine, a button in your word processor - these look like different products, but they are the same LLM technology aimed at different tasks. That is why the skills in this course transfer everywhere: once you understand how to work with the model underneath, you recognize it in every shape it takes.

## Free vs. Paid

Most AI tools offer a free tier and a paid one. Knowing the difference saves you from both overpaying and from judging the technology by a weaker version of it.

## What Paid Usually Buys

The free tier is often enough to get real work done, and it is the right place to start. Paying usually unlocks:

- **Stronger models.** The paid plan typically gives you access to the most capable model, which is noticeably better at hard tasks.
- **Higher limits.** More messages, longer inputs, fewer "come back later" walls.
- **Extra features.** Things like working with files, images, or connected tools.

If your free-tier experience felt underwhelming, there is a good chance you were using a smaller model than the one people rave about.

## How to Choose

A simple approach works for most people:

1. Start with a free tier of one of the major assistants and use it for real tasks.
2. When a task is important or hard, reach for the strongest model you have access to.
3. If you find yourself using it every day and hitting limits, that is the signal to consider paying.

Do not agonize over picking the single "best" tool. They are close, they leapfrog each other constantly, and the skill of asking well matters far more than the brand.
